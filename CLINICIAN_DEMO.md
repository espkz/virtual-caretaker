# Scenario 2: AI hospice nurse

This is a separate reversed-role version of the hospice communication scenario:

- **AI:** Margaret's home-hospice registered nurse.
- **Human:** Rachel, Margaret's daughter and family caregiver.
- **Margaret:** the patient, present but unable to communicate. Her clinical condition is unchanged.

The original two AI-Rachel scenarios remain available. This demonstration lets the human ask questions and experience the nurse's explanations, rather than practice answering an AI family member.

## Try it locally

The new draft has been imported into this checkout. Log in as an instructor, open **Test Chat**, and select **Scenario 2: AI hospice nurse (you play Rachel)**. Press **New Chat** and ask a question as Rachel. For example:

> My brother is just giving up on her. How can he decide this when I'm the one caring for her?

You can also begin with feeding, suffering, or morphine questions. The nurse answers the first question directly; it does not emit Rachel's old opening line. Instructors can test drafts without releasing them. To make this version visible to student accounts, activate it in **Prompts** after reviewing it.

## Behavior and implementation

`prompts/role_hospice_nurse_2.md` declares `## Simulation Mode` as `clinician_demo`. The prompt editor exposes that choice and preserves it when saving. Mode and case text live in the session's existing scenario snapshot, so no new database migration is needed. The choice does not automatically rewrite a patient's role or dialogue: the Role, User Role, guidance, and closing must also describe the intended clinician scenario.

`vip/clinician_demo.py` generates nurse dialogue with its own role and grounding instructions. It bypasses both the daughter question-selection path and the older heuristics that reject nurse-like speech. It does not load the family-mode global prompt, whose instructions conflict with clinician explanations. The scenario supplies the case facts, clinical communication guidance, and three discussion objectives.

The nurse answers the human's concerns in their chosen order, uses short explanations, and checks understanding/readiness. Discussion coverage persists between requests and is not a competency score or proof of consent. Normal completion requires all three discussion themes, a previous nurse readiness check, and an explicit readiness signal from Rachel without an outstanding question. Simple acknowledgements such as “thanks” do not count. Pausing can end the conversation before all themes are discussed; the shared 20-submission cap has a nurse-appropriate closing. The model still interprets intent and coverage, so these judgments can be imperfect.

The scenario ends before pump operation or technical medication teaching. The output adapter blocks recognizable daughter dialogue, medication units, and common dose/interval instructions. Model-reported unsupported content is replaced with a response explaining that the care team needs to check the detail. These checks supplement the prompt; they do not guarantee detection of every invented fact or unsafe statement. Unlike the core-question path, this mode generates new spoken text and needs faculty review of its answers.

The clinical and decision-making facts come from the supplied Scenario 2 Word documents, including their hidden role guidance, and apply to this fictional case. General end-of-life communication and symptom-care context was cross-checked against the [NCI patient summary](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/advanced-cancer/caregivers/planning/last-days-pdq). That source concerns advanced cancer and does not independently validate Margaret's brain-injury case, the case's legal assertions, or the model's responses. No medication prescription is supplied or generated by the intended workflow.

## Verification

The expanded offline suite passes 40 tests, including 17 clinician-mode tests covering role selection, first-question handling, mode round trips, nurse speech previously caught by family-mode guards, readiness, pauses, stop commands, progress persistence, invalid output, role/dose boundaries, imports, and editing. Both real Chrome tests pass, including selecting the inactive nurse draft, sending as Rachel, and reaching the nurse's closing.

The live smoke test completed a seven-submission nurse conversation and two independent role-switch/dose-request checks. The seven model-backed replies took approximately 1.5–3.5 seconds. A polite acknowledgement kept the conversation open; explicit readiness after the nurse's check closed it. The role-switch attempt produced nurse dialogue, and the medication request produced the prescription/teaching boundary response. This small run verifies integration, not clinical validity or behavior across all student inputs.
