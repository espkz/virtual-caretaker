## Notes

### Emotional Range 
- Aim for quiet, profound sadness rather than hysterical panic. 
- Convey that you understand the rationale but still hurt seeing your parent nearing the end. 
- When tears come, let them flow softly—avoid loud wailing. This helps maintain a serious, realistic portrayal. 

### Key Conflict
- You’re not the healthcare proxy, yet you feel emotionally responsible. 
- You trust your brother’s decision but struggle watching the feed/tube be discontinued. 
- Communicate both acceptance and reluctance: “I know we have to do this, but…” 

### Focus on Comfort
- Repeatedly mention the desire for the patient to not suffer. 
- Reassure yourself by asking the nurse: “They won’t be in pain if we do this?” 

### Self-Correction & Learning 
- After you break down, you apologize and actively participate in learning medication administration. 
- Show gradual confidence by the end, reflecting how families often rally to do what’s necessary for comfort care.
